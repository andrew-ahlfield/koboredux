# Theme catalog

Everything the Kobo Redux engine asks a graphics or sound theme for, and
where the original **Halcyon** themes stand on each item. Halcyon is the
default theme pair (`GFX>>halcyon`, `SFX>>halcyon`, set in `src/config.h`);
`GFX>>mono` and `SFX>>chip` remain the fallbacks.

Status values:

- **done**: provided by Halcyon.
- **silent (by design)**: a sound slot Halcyon leaves undefined on purpose,
  because another slot already covers the moment. The engine treats every
  sound slot as optional. The `chip` theme leaves the same slots empty.
- **not used**: declared by the engine but not read by any game theme (the
  fallback `mono` theme does not provide it either).
- **kept**: provided by an existing theme that Halcyon does not replace.

All Halcyon art is generated by `tools/art/halcyon` (see
`tools/art/README.md`); the music is generated by
`tools/audio/halcyon_music.py`. Both themes are CC BY-SA 4.0.

### Region tiles and backgrounds

Five regions, each with its own directory `region-1` to `region-5` and its
own material: naval gunmetal with cyan lights, desert khaki with rust trim
and green lights, olive drab with yellow hazard paint, plum armour with
gold trim and pink lights, white ceramic with red markings. Each region's
reactor cores glow in a hue that region uses nowhere else on a base: hot
pink (1), electric blue (2), magenta (3), aqua (4) and green (5).

Bases are mazes on a two cell grid (junction cells on even positions,
straight cells between them, the odd/odd cells always empty), so a thin
corridor per cell reads as pipework. Halcyon draws each cell as a broad
hull slab instead: it fills the tile edge to edge on every connected
side, stops one pixel short on closed sides with chamfered corners, and
shows a three pixel south wall, so the empty cells read as trenches
between armoured blocks. The look varies by neighbour count:

- Straight cells (masks 5 and 10, variants 40 to 45) are low, darker
  wall walk decks: two armour plates per cell, a bulkhead joint where
  cells meet and a narrow sunken rail channel. Variants add a raised
  bulkhead with hazard chevrons, a flush intake grille with a running
  light, or an inset access hatch.
- Corners, tees and crosses carry a tower: a bright roof seven rows tall
  with a four pixel wall of lit windows and a drop shadow onto the deck,
  topped by a fan and grille, a command dome, a roof grille with an
  exhaust stack, a stepped second storey or a radar dish (picked from the
  mask and region). Junction heavy clusters therefore read as a fortress
  of towers joined by wall walks.
- Single bit masks are twin barrel turrets on a domed gun house; hard end
  nodes are hazard striped bunkers.
- Cores are the player's target and are drawn to be the first thing the
  eye lands on: a round armoured drum that overflows the deck (the only
  round silhouette that big on a base) holding a nine pixel glassy orb in
  the region's reactor hue, with a white hot heart, a hard specular glint
  and a bright crescent of inner reflection, floating in a black socket
  inside a bright energy band. The rim catches the reactor light and the
  feeds glow in the rail channel. Over the 8 frame loop the orb and band
  throb from deep to white hot and four dark clamps turn a quarter turn
  around the band.
- The engine has no damaged or destroyed core frames: it draws
  `16 + (game_time / 2) % 8` (or 24 + ...) whatever the core's health, a
  damaged core spits `PFX_CORE_FIRE` sparks more often the lower its
  health, and a destroyed core's cell is cleared and its base blown apart
  by the pipe chain, leaving the wreckage stubs. Halcyon's
  `PFX_CORE_FIRE` is hot and sparky so a hurt core visibly burns.

Tile sheets are 8x7 frames: 0 is the alternate 4 way junction (landing
pad); 1 to 15 are indexed by connection mask (up 1, right 2, down 4,
left 8; single bits are cannon turrets); 16 to 23 animate the core
vertically and 24 to 31 horizontally; 32 to 35 are hard nodes (up, right,
down, left); 40 to 45 are straight variants (even frames vertical); 48 to
55 are wreckage stubs.

The 12 and 8 px parallax sets are drawn separately as distant
silhouettes, not shrunk from the 16 px art: flat dark deck masses with a
lit north west rim, towers as the bright masses with a row of lit
windows, turrets as small domes with a light. Window lights are three by
two blocks on the box filter's grid so they keep full brightness through
the downscale and stay visible after the theme's `.38` and `.6` layer
dimming. Far cores are scenery, not targets: a dark round housing with a
small dim glow no brighter than a window. No seams, rails or rivets, so
the far layers read as distant structures with lights rather than as
more maze. `python3 -m halcyon.mazepreview` builds
engine style mazes with the tiles for a quick look at all three sizes.

## Sound

Programs live in `data/sfx/halcyon`: `master.a2s` (mixer groups),
`sfx.a2s` (game effects), `uisfx.a2s` (user interface), `music.a2s`
(generated). Effects take `(pitch volume pan)`; continuous effects also
handle `1()` stop, `2(Dmg)` damage and `3(npP npV npX Dur)` position.

## What is left

Nothing the engine reads is missing from either Halcyon theme. Possible
future work, none of it required:

- Distinct sprites for `B_ROCK2` and `B_ROCK3` (currently aliases of
  `B_ROCK1`, which `mono` also does).
- Separate particle definitions for each enemy kind (now aliases of
  `PFX_ENEMY1`).
- Longer arrangements for the music: each track is one eight bar
  progression arranged in three to four sections.
